How it works

Three steps, no guessing.

MaterialIQ screens metals the way you would by hand, then shows its working so you can check it.

1. Screen

Each metal is checked against every limit you set. To pass, its value must meet the limit. For strength and elongation that is the lowest published value across sources (A- or S-basis where the handbook gives one). Density is a typical value.

2. Flag gaps

If a value is missing, or the data was measured at a different temperature, the metal goes to "Needs data". The handbook values are room temperature (recorded as 22 °C) and count for operating temperatures from 12 to 32 °C.

3. Rank

Metals that pass are ranked on what you picked under "Rank by". Each goal is scaled from worst to best among the passing metals and weighted equally. If the top two are within 5%, it says there's no single answer.

AboutMaterialIQ is a free early-access tool built by a mechanical engineer to make material screening traceable. It is being built in the open, and feedback decides what comes next.
DataProperties come from MIL-HDBK-5J (2003), a US Government handbook approved for public release. It has been superseded by MMPDS, so values may differ from current data. Use it as a reference for screening, not as a design allowable.
Privacy and termsThe values you enter stay in your browser. Only anonymous page and button counts are recorded. MaterialIQ is advisory only and comes with no warranty. Verify every value before use in a design. Contact us.
